# Full audit — 2026-08-31

Scope, per Austin: re-verify every promise the app makes (the D-022 class of
bug), a critical UI/UX pass, and recommendations to make this a killer app —
informed by the competitive deep dive (`docs/COMPETITIVE-2026-08-31.md`).
Audited on branch `skins` (quiet default + satellite default + D-022/023).

---

## Part 1 · Promises vs reality

Method: enumerate every outbound host in `lib/`, every permission in the
merged manifest, and every user-facing claim (save toast, settings copy,
onboarding, the public privacy page), then check each claim against code.

### The egress inventory (current, complete)

| Host | Purpose | Gate | Honest? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 127.0.0.1 loopback | offline tile server | none needed | ✓ |
| build.protomaps.com | basemap download | explicit download + NetworkPolicy | ✓ |
| basemap.nationalmap.gov | satellite tiles | **viewing the map (now default-on)** | ⚠ P1 below |

No analytics, crash-reporting, or tracking SDK exists in the dependency
tree. The Privacy rows in Settings ("Account: none", "Analytics: off — no
record content, no coordinates, ever") are true. D-022's guarantees hold
under test: zero requests while off; full-precision coordinates never on
the wire.

### Findings

**P1 · Satellite-by-default quietly weakens privacy case 4 — fix wording
before `skins` merges.** The privacy page says tile requests happen "when
you download an offline basemap" and frames the satellite layer as a thing
you switch on. As of this branch, satellite is the standard view: opening
the Map tab with signal requests USGS tiles, which necessarily reveals the
viewport — before the user has done anything. The traffic itself is
defensible (public-domain federal tile server, no identity, no records
attached) and it's what Austin asked for; but the page's "every case only
happens because you asked" framing is no longer exactly true. Fix: rewrite
case 4 to say imagery loads whenever the map is on screen and can be turned
off in Layers. One paragraph. *(Also note: raster tiles bypass the D-016
cellular policy — imagery browses freely on LTE while basemap downloads
politely wait for Wi-Fi. Either gate imagery when `allowCellular` is off,
or say plainly that map imagery uses mobile data.)*

**P2 · The Settings version row is hardcoded** (`'1.1.0+2'` in
`settings_tab.dart`) and will silently drift from `pubspec.yaml` on the
next bump. It's now load-bearing (the easter egg lives on it). Fix:
`package_info_plus`, five minutes.

**P3 · Restore path unverified against Drive.** Backup to Drive is proven
on-device; a *restore* from Drive has never been walked end-to-end. An
untested restore is not a backup — the app's own banner says so. Needs one
deliberate drill: wipe → restore from Drive → verify records + media.

Everything else checked clean: the save toast's "nothing has left the
phone" is record-scoped and true; onboarding's backup page now matches
shipped reality (D-021); LAN receiver only ever holds ciphertext; the
consent screen shows `drive.appdata` alone; version-row unlock persists.

## Part 2 · UI/UX — the critical pass

Findings ranked. U1–U3 are the ones I'd block a public release on.

**U1 · Species identification is buried.** The whole flow exists — Pl@ntNet
+ LLM + suggestions retained + user acceptance — but the *only* entry point
is record detail → identify. At capture time, where iNaturalist has taught
every phone-owner to expect a suggestion, we offer nothing. You must save,
find the record, open it, and ask. Fix: an "identify" affordance on the
capture form's species field (and/or on the save toast: "Saved · identify
this?"). The plumbing is done; this is one button.

**U2 · Quiet skin still shouts.** Button and label copy is uppercase
literals ("BACK UP NOW", "TAKE MY DATA — FULL EXPORT") rendered in system
type — reads like a form, not like the calm contemporary default we chose.
Known phase-2 debt (D-023), but it's the single biggest tell that quiet is
a reskin. Fix: route label copy through `skin.label()` so press keeps its
caps and quiet gets sentence case.

**U3 · No dark mode.** Quiet is light-only. The contemporary standard
Austin invoked (Apple HIG) treats dark as table stakes, and dusk is real
field time. The skin seam makes this genuinely cheap now: a `quietDark`
skin is a token set, not a redesign. Press can stay light-only forever —
paper is paper.

**U4 · Settings has three homes for backup.** Status card at top, a
"Backup" group (computer/Drive) mid-list, and automatic-backup under
Network. A new user cannot form a model of "is my stuff safe" from one
place. Fold into one Backup section: status, destinations, cadence.

**U5 · The capture FAB has no accessibility label** (uiautomator shows
`NAF=true`). One `Semantics(label: 'New capture')` wrapper. While in
there: the map-chrome pills have labels, the tab bar reads fine — this is
the only naked control I found.

**U6 · Empty states are thin.** Grow/Species/Ledger on a fresh property
show blank lists with no "here's what goes here" hand. First-run UX runs on
momentum from onboarding; one line + one action per empty tab ("No plantings
yet — record your first batch") would carry it.

**U7 · Small frictions:** GPS pill can show a stale ±95 m long after the
fix improves (accuracy updates only on new fixes — consider fading stale
values); outdoor mode is buried in Settings→Display though its audience is
someone squinting in sun (surface it in map chrome or quick settings);
species free-text with auto-suggest is right per D-004, but there's no
"recently used" shortlist at capture, which is where gloved thumbs live.

## Part 3 · The killer-app recommendations

The moat is already built: offline + no account + owned data + encrypted
backup + lineage + photo points. Nobody on the competitive list has that
spine, and the cloud players *can't* copy it. What's missing is the layer
that makes the moat *visible* and the daily loop *magnetic*.

**Tier 1 — finish the promise (do before any public hand-off):**
1. P1–P3 + U1–U3 above.
2. The evidence packet (from the steward review): one tap → PDF of a
   practice's records, photos, dates, survival — formatted for an NRCS/TPWD
   desk. Programs data already exists (M6). *No competitor has this; it is
   the single most monetisable feature in the plan.*

**Tier 2 — steal what fits (competitive doc, ranked by fit):**
3. Monitoring protocols (Plot Hound's soul): saved spot + form + cadence,
   feeding photo points and survival. Turns casual walks into defensible
   monitoring.
4. "Soil under me" browser (SoilWeb's trick) reusing the D-022 SDA client
   and its privacy gate — makes the opt-in worth switching on.
5. Pocket instruments (TX BMPs): clinometer slope + tree height from phone
   sensors. Offline, delightful, an afternoon each.
6. iNaturalist export (opt-in, per record, obscured coords): distribution
   channel disguised as a feature.
7. Long Watch integration (WoodsApp's product, our pipeline): zone NDVI
   change alerts from Austin's own Sentinel-2 instrument. Even a monthly
   "Section 04 is down 18% vs last August" row in the app would be a
   category first for private owners.

**Tier 3 — the daily loop (steward proposals, still the right list):**
8. Phenology chips on capture ("flowering", "fruiting", "leaf-out") — two
   taps that build the property's calendar.
9. "This week" surface: what happened here this week in past years, what's
   due (from protocols + programs). The return-visit principle, made a
   home screen.
10. Seed lots + collection-as-capture (needs the schema decision that's
    been parked).

**What NOT to build:** our own cloud, our own species CV model, team/multi-
user sync ahead of M4's design, or anything that requires our server to
exist. Every competitor with a server is one subscription lapse away from
losing the customer's history. "Your land's memory, on your phone, forever"
only stays true if we stay out of the loop.

---

## Verdict

The app's promises now check out — the one real gap (P1) is wording drift
caused by a change made *today*, caught the same day. The product gap is no
longer trust or plumbing; it's reach: identification hidden one screen too
deep, the quiet skin one pass from feeling native, and the moat features
(evidence packet, protocols, Long Watch) still in the drawer. Tier 1 makes
it shippable to strangers. Tier 2 makes it the only serious answer in its
category. Tier 3 makes it something you open every morning with coffee.
